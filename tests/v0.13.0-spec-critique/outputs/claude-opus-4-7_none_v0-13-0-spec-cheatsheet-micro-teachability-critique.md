## Question 1 — Composition rules clarity

Rule 4 is the weakest of the five. The phrase "flex-grow semantics" is a hand-wave to readers who *do* know Flutter/CSS, and an opaque incantation to those who don't. The walkthrough that follows ("siblings split available space equally until the capped sibling hits its cap and freezes; remaining space is redistributed proportionally among uncapped `fill: true` siblings") is better, but "proportionally" is doing unearned work. Proportional to what? With only `fill: true` (no weight property mentioned anywhere in these artifacts), the answer is presumably "equally among the remaining uncapped siblings" — which is what "proportionally" reduces to when all weights are 1. If Igni has no flex-weight concept, just say "split equally" twice and drop "proportionally"; if it does have weights, the spec needs to mention them or point to where they live. The current wording leaves an LLM free to invent a `flex: 2` property to satisfy "proportionally." Also unstated: what happens if a capped sibling's cap is *smaller* than its equal share would have been — does it still freeze, releasing slack? (Presumably yes, but the rule's "until the capped sibling hits its cap" implies the cap is reached by growing, not by being below the equal share at start.) An explicit two-sibling worked example with pixel numbers would shrink the rule from a paragraph to a glance.

## Question 2 — Box model (rule 5)

Rule 5 is communicated well. The "480px wide *including* the 24px padding on each side — the inner content area is 432px" sentence does the arithmetic in-line (480 − 24 − 24 = 432), and the explicit pin to CSS `box-sizing: border-box` gives readers with web background a one-shot anchor. The one nit: "24px padding" appears without prior establishment that `padding: large` = 24px. A reader scanning only this section sees a pixel value materialize from a token they were just told *not* to think about as pixels. A parenthetical "(`large` = 24px; see §Spacing tokens)" would close the loop. For LLM accuracy specifically, this is a place where the artifact teaches a token-to-pixel mapping inline, which is good — but it should be flagged as exemplary, not load-bearing, lest a model conclude `large` always means 24px in every context (which it presumably does, but the cross-reference would confirm it).

## Question 3 — Cheatsheet density

I'd add the second example. The cheatsheet paragraph is doing a lot — five distinct claims compressed into five sentences (token list, omission canonicality, no-numeric, box-model, fill composition) — and the existing MiCard example only demonstrates the first and arguably the box-model claim implicitly. The composition-with-`fill` claim is the highest-variance behavior (it's where the spec spent four of its five rules) and the example slot is silent on it. A two-line example showing one capped + one uncapped `fill: true` sibling would let a scanning reader confirm the redistribution behavior without context-switching to the full spec. The density cost is real, but cheatsheets earn their keep on the hardest-to-remember interactions, not the easiest. MiCard is memorable from the spec already; the redistribution case isn't.

## Question 4 — Micro and the absent `full` token

The hint lands for a careful reader but is fragile under LLM autocomplete pressure. "Omit for uncapped (no `full` token)" negates *one* of at least three plausible hallucinations — `none`, `auto`, and `full` are all idiomatic in CSS-adjacent ecosystems, and the micro only inoculates against `full`. An LLM that has internalized "the spec said no `full`" may still cheerfully emit `max_width: none` or `max_width: auto` because those weren't named. The spec's prose handles this better ("Don't introduce a `none` or `full` token") but the micro is the artifact most likely to be the *only* thing in an LLM's context window for a small completion. I'd tighten to "omit for uncapped — no `full` / `none` / `auto`" or, more aggressively, "the three tokens above are exhaustive; omission is the fourth state." The token-first discipline is precisely the thing that fails silently when a model guesses an alias, so the micro is the wrong place to economize.

## Question 5 — Wildcard

Three things caught me. **First**, none of the artifacts say what `max_width:` does on a `horizontal` layout. The MiCard example, the micro, and the box-model walkthrough all use `vertical`. Rule 5's "outer rendered layout box" is direction-agnostic in principle, but a reader could reasonably wonder whether `max_width:` on a horizontal row caps the row's overall width (presumably yes) or has some interaction with how children lay out along the main axis. One clarifying sentence would close this. **Second**, rule 3 ("parent narrower than the cap, `max_width:` is a no-op") is correct but the word "no-op" is slightly misleading — `max_width:` is still doing the work of *not constraining further*, which is different from being absent. The behavioral outcome is the same as omission in that one geometry, but a reader debugging a responsive layout might want to know the property remains live and will re-engage if the parent grows past the cap. "Inactive at this width" reads more accurately than "no-op." **Third**, the spec says users "cannot write `max_width: 540`" but doesn't say what the compiler does — error? warning? silent round-to-nearest? For a language pitching LLM accuracy, the failure mode of the most likely LLM mistake (emitting a number) deserves one sentence. "Compile error" is the answer I'd guess, but guessing is exactly what the spec is trying to eliminate.
